# Raleway Studio — Version 1.1 Photoshoot Brief

**Role:** Creative Director's shoot brief, written for a photographer to execute without a follow-up call.
**Subject:** Seira, co-founder, Raleway Studio.
**Duration:** half-day, 4 hours on location + 30 minutes setup/wrap.
**Best window:** shoot in the 2–3 hours after sunrise or the 2–3 hours before sunset — soft, low-angle, warm natural light, which is the entire lighting philosophy below. Avoid a midday call time; if the only available slot is midday, see the Contingencies section for the overcast/open-shade fallback.
**Deliverables this shoot must produce** (cross-referenced to `RALEWAY-V1.1-ASSET-CHECKLIST.md`): Asset #16 (Founder Portrait — Secondary Crop), #18 (Texture/Material Stills), #19 (Environment Establishing Shot), plus raw material toward #10 (Home Breath Image — the annotated-page/hands shots).

---

## Ground truth — match this exactly

Raleway Studio already has one real, live, approved photograph: `public/assets/seira-jho.jpg`, on the About page today. This shoot is not a new visual direction — it is a continuation and extension of that exact photograph's language. Before anything else, the photographer should study that image and treat it as the calibration reference for colour, light quality, and warmth. Specifically, match:

- **Light quality and direction:** soft, directional, warm-toned light falling from camera-left, striking the subject at a gentle angle — not flat, not frontal, not overhead.
- **Colour temperature:** warm throughout, no blue or cool cast anywhere in the shadows. This is the single most important technical target and the most common way a shoot drifts off-brand — see Camera Settings, White Balance, below.
- **Background texture:** a warm, vertically-slatted wood surface alternating between a cooler grey-toned slat and a warm honey-toned slat, softly out of focus behind the subject.
- **Overall mood:** warm, approachable, genuinely smiling — not a stiff corporate headshot, not an overly serious editorial pose. The existing photo's warmth is a brand asset; this shoot should produce more frames in that same emotional register, plus a smaller set of quieter, more contemplative frames for variety (see Poses & Expressions).

## Wardrobe

Three looks, styled from the subject's own wardrobe where possible — nothing should read as "shoot costume." Steam or press everything the night before; no visible wrinkles.

**Look A — Primary (continuity look).** A cropped or relaxed knit cardigan in burnt clay-orange (matching the existing photo almost exactly — if the same cardigan is still available, use it), worn over a plain cream or off-white top (not bright white — bright white will blow out against the site's parchment tones). Bottom: wide-leg trousers in camel, oat, or warm taupe. Same glasses as the existing photo if unchanged (silver/gunmetal thin frame). Minimal jewellery: one thin gold ring is enough, nothing else — no necklaces or earrings that could catch light and distract.

**Look B — Secondary (no orange in wardrobe).** All warm-neutral: an oat or cream knit sweater, warm grey or camel trousers, same glasses. The orange accent for any shot in this look comes from a prop instead (see Props) — never both wardrobe and prop orange in the same frame.

**Look C — Working detail look.** A plain, slightly oversized cream or white cotton button-up, sleeves rolled to the forearm. Used only for close hand/desk shots where the face isn't in frame, so fit and styling matter less than fabric texture and colour reading correctly in close-up.

**Wardrobe exclusions — do not bring or wear:** busy patterns, any visible logos or graphic text, true black (too cold against this palette), bright/optical white, cool tones (blue, teal, green, purple), glossy or shiny synthetic fabrics, anything with metallic thread or sequins.

## Colour palette (for stylist, set dressing, and prop pulling)

- **Neutrals (majority of every frame):** oat, parchment, warm taupe, camel, warm grey. Think unbleached linen, raw wood, aged paper.
- **Grounding dark (used sparingly — one element per frame, max):** warm near-black, like the site's `#1A1A18` — a dark trouser or a single dark object, never the dominant tone of a frame.
- **The one accent (one per frame, maximum, ever):** burnt clay-orange, close to `#E05C1A` — a genuine burnt orange, not a bright pumpkin or safety orange. This shows up as either the Look A cardigan or a single prop object — never as two things in the same frame, and never in Look B portraits at all unless it's the one prop.
- **Explicitly forbidden anywhere in frame:** blues, teals, greens, purples, hot pinks, pastels, true white, true black used as a dominant colour.

## Locations

Three set-ups within a single location, planned to minimise moves and re-lighting during the half-day window.

**Location 1 — Primary portrait wall.** The same warm wood-slat wall visible in the existing reference photo, or, if that was a temporary setup, a close match: vertical wood slats alternating warm and slightly cooler tones, positioned so a large window can light the subject from roughly 45° to camera-left while they stand or sit 2–3 feet in front of the wall (enough distance for the wall to fall out of focus at wide apertures).

**Location 2 — Desk / workspace corner.** A real desk with a warm wood or warm-neutral surface, positioned beside a window. This is where all hand/materials/detail shots happen, and where the Environment Establishing Shot (Asset #19) is framed. If the everyday desk has visible cool-toned or branded objects on it (laptop stickers, cables, mugs from other companies), clear them before shooting — see Props for what should replace them.

**Location 3 — Quiet corner, negative space.** A simple, uncluttered wall corner near a window, with room for a single plain chair, used for the wide environment shot and the "implied presence, no person" style frames.

**Backup/indoor fallback:** if natural light is unusable on the shoot day (heavy overcast turning grey-blue, or unexpected harsh midday sun with no way to diffuse it), identify one additional interior spot with the largest available window and the most neutral-toned wall as a fallback before the shoot day, so no time is lost scouting on the day itself.

## Camera & technical settings

- **Body:** full-frame mirrorless or DSLR, shooting RAW only (not JPEG) — colour grading happens in post to hit the exact brand palette, and RAW is required for that latitude.
- **White balance:** set manually, not auto. Take a reading off a grey card at the start of each set-up, then dial 200–300K *warmer* than the neutral reading — the brand target is deliberately warmer than "accurate," and auto white balance on most cameras will drift cool the moment a cloud passes over, which is the single most common way a shoot goes off-brand. Check the LCD histogram and blinkies periodically to confirm shadows aren't reading blue.
- **Metering:** spot or centre-weighted for portraits, exposing for the subject's skin — let the background fall where it falls. Evaluative/matrix metering is fine for the wider environment and still-life shots.
- **Aperture:**
  - Portraits (Location 1): f/1.8–f/2.8 — shallow depth of field, subject sharp, wall soft.
  - Hand/detail/still-life shots: f/2.8–f/4 — enough depth that the working hand and the page it's touching are both sharp.
  - Wide environment shots (Location 3): f/4–f/5.6 — the room should read as a coherent space, not an isolated blur.
- **Shutter speed:** minimum 1/125s handheld for any portrait with the subject moving or talking; 1/60–1/125s acceptable for locked-down tripod still-life work. Never below 1/60s handheld.
- **ISO:** base ISO (100–200) wherever light allows, especially for tripod-based still life. Portraits can run to ISO 400–800 in lower light — keep noise invisible at normal viewing size; if it's visible, add light rather than pushing ISO further.
- **Focus:** single-point AF on the eye nearest camera for every portrait. Manual focus with focus peaking for still-life macro work.

## Lenses

Bring four primes (or their zoom equivalents at the matching focal length, aperture allowing):

- **35mm, f/1.4–2.8** — wide environment shots, the workspace-corner establishing shot.
- **50mm, f/1.4–1.8** — general-purpose portrait and hand/desk shots, the workhorse lens for most of the day.
- **85mm, f/1.8** — tighter portraits, compressed background, the primary lens for the Founder Portrait alternate.
- **90–100mm macro** — still life and texture detail: annotated pages, paper stock, fabric close-ups.

These four focal lengths are the same ones already specified for the site's AI-generated supporting imagery (`RALEWAY-AI-IMAGE-PROMPTS-v1.1.md`) — using the same lens language keeps every image on the site, generated or photographed, reading as one coherent visual system.

## Lighting

Natural window light is the primary and, ideally, only light source — this is a brand commitment, not a budget shortcut (see the Ground Truth section). Bring gear to control and supplement it, not replace it.

**Key:** the window itself. Position the subject 3–6 feet back from the glass, light striking from roughly 45° to camera-left (matching the reference photo). If the sun is direct and hard, diffuse it with a 6x6 or 4x4 silk/scrim clipped or stood in the window — the goal is the soft, wrapped quality of open shade, not a hard-edged sunbeam.

**Fill:** one 5-in-1 reflector, white or silver side only (not gold — gold pushes an uneven, artificial warmth on top of the grading that happens in post). Position camera-right, low, bounced back into the shadow side of the face, just enough to keep shadow detail without flattening the light. The brand wants visible, gentle modelling on the face — do not fill to the point of flatness.

**Supplement/backup (only if natural light is insufficient — see Contingencies):** one continuous LED panel or one strobe through a large softbox, colour-corrected to approximately 4800K, always through diffusion, positioned to replicate the window's angle and softness. Never used bare, never used as a second competing direction of light.

**Explicitly do not use:** on-camera flash or speedlight fired directly at the subject, ring lights (the flat, circular catchlight they produce doesn't match anything else on the site), coloured gels beyond warm colour-correction, any hard undiffused light source.

## Poses & expressions

Direct two registers across the day, both genuine — this brand's photography works because it doesn't look performed.

**Register 1 — Warm engagement (continuity with the existing photo).** Direct eye contact with the lens, a real smile — not a held, toothy "say cheese" smile, but the kind that comes from actually talking to the photographer between frames. Shoulders relaxed, weight on one leg if standing, hands doing something real (holding the mug, resting on the desk) rather than posed at the sides. Get this register first, while energy is highest.

**Register 2 — Quiet focus (new, for variety and future use — bylines, press, a more editorial context).** Three-quarter angle or profile, gaze directed off-frame rather than at the lens, an unposed, mid-thought expression — caught adjusting a page, caught mid-sentence looking away, caught in a genuine pause. Camera at eye level or very slightly below. This register should feel like a candid moment captured, not a portrait sitting.

**For both registers, explicitly avoid:** the crossed-arms "confident founder" pose, hand-on-chin "thinking" pose, direct-to-camera pointing or presenting gestures, an overly wide performative smile held for multiple frames, stiff hands (give the subject something real to hold or touch in every standing/seated frame).

**Hand/detail shots specifically:** natural mid-motion — adjusting a page, holding a pen just above the paper as if about to write, resting on the desk edge, holding the mug with both hands. Nothing posed to look like a hand model shot; slight motion blur on a moving hand at 1/125s is acceptable and often more honest than a perfectly frozen, obviously staged gesture.

## Backup shots / contingencies

- **Every numbered shot-list item should have at least one backup frame** at a slightly different crop or angle, shot immediately after the primary — insurance against blinks, motion blur, or a expression that reads wrong in review, without needing a re-shoot.
- **Weather contingency:** if the scheduled morning is heavily overcast (flat, blue-grey light) or unexpectedly bright and harsh, move indoors to the Location 1 backup spot identified during pre-shoot scouting, and rely on the supplemental LED/strobe-through-softbox setup described in Lighting.
- **Wardrobe contingency:** if Look A doesn't photograph well against the wall (colour clash, pattern moiré, whatever the reason), Look B becomes primary and the single orange prop carries the accent instead — this is exactly why Look B was designed to work independently rather than as a lesser backup.
- **Expression contingency:** if Register 1 (warm engagement) isn't landing naturally after a few minutes, stop directing expression entirely and shoot Register 2 first instead — genuine quiet focus is easier to get on demand than a forced smile, and returning to Register 1 later once the subject has settled in usually works better than pushing through.
- **Extra still-life variations:** shoot at least two different arrangements for Set-up 5 (Asset #18) even though only one is strictly needed — this is the cheapest insurance in the whole shoot, since it requires no subject, no wardrobe, and can be done in spare minutes between other set-ups.

## Post-production handoff notes

- **Colour grade target:** highlights toward `#F6F4EF` (warm oat, not clinical white), shadows toward `#1A1A18` (warm near-black, not blue-black or grey-black) — this is the same grade specified across every generated image in `RALEWAY-AI-IMAGE-PROMPTS-v1.1.md`; the photographed and generated assets should be indistinguishable in palette when placed on the same page.
- **Grain:** fine, filmic — if shooting a high-ISO frame that reads digitally noisy, that's a technical flaw to fix (re-shoot with more light), not a look to lean into. The desired "grain" is closer to Portra 400 at box speed than to visible digital noise.
- **No selective saturation boosts** on the orange accent — it should read as true-to-life burnt orange, not an oversaturated pop.
- **Delivery specs**, matching `RALEWAY-V1.1-ASSET-CHECKLIST.md`:
  - Founder Portrait alternate (#16): deliver at minimum 1600 × 2000px, 4:5 crop, plus the full uncropped RAW/master.
  - Texture/Material stills (#18): deliver at minimum 1600 × 1600px, square-cropped source with room to re-crop.
  - Environment shot (#19): deliver at minimum 1600 × 900px, 16:9 crop, plus the full uncropped master.
  - Home Breath Image candidates (feeds #10): deliver the strongest 2–3 frames from Set-up 3 at minimum 2400 × 1350px, 16:9 crop, plus uncropped masters — final selection happens after review, not on shoot day.
- **File format:** RAW (camera native) as the archival master for every frame; JPEG or WebP exports at the dimensions above, quality 85–90, for anything headed directly to production.
